## 1. Baseline: playable game, five content packs

**What**
- Vite + vanilla TypeScript app with no UI framework, built to static files.
- The canvas pixel-art scene (`src/render/scene.ts`) has a torch-lit shaft, a bonfire with a
  coiled sword, ash/ember particles, banded pixel lighting, a knight that sinks
  as you score, and red eyes deeper down.
- Synthesized WebAudio sound effects (`src/render/sfx.ts`).
- Screens:
  - home (bonfire)
  - Daily Descent
  - Themed Catacombs (4 themes + 3 sealed)
  - Archive (practice)
  - game
  - results with emoji share
  - how-to-play and settings modals
- i18n for EN, PL and UK, auto-detected and switchable.
- The answer engine (`src/engine/normalize.ts`, `match.ts`):
  - Cyrillic transliteration, diacritic stripping and phonetic folding
  - Roman numerals, typo tolerance by length, and a per-prompt reject list
  - exact spelling takes priority, which keeps кіт (cat) and кит (whale) apart
- Daily deal: a weighted rotation across packs with seeded per-pack decks, so no repeat
  within 9 days. A run in progress is pinned to its prompt ids, and reloading
  counts the current prompt as lost.
- Content written by agents and checked by `npm run validate`:

## 8. Offline play (service worker) + idle pack prefetch

**What**
- `public/sw.js` is a small hand-written service worker with no library:
  - hashed `/assets/*` are served cache-first, since they never change
  - pages are network-first with a cache fallback, so new deploys show up immediately
  - old caches are deleted on activate
- It is registered only in production builds.
- After first paint, every content pack is loaded in `requestIdleCallback`. The daily
  starts instantly, and the service worker has everything cached for offline play.

**Verified:** Playwright with the context switched offline after one online
visit. `#/daily` still renders its first prompt. `tsc` and the build are clean.

## 10. Audio only after a gesture; resilient content loading

**What**
- Audio: no `AudioContext` is created until the first pointer or key gesture, and it is
  created inside that gesture. This removes the browser's autoplay warnings, for example on
  a first visit when How-to-play opens automatically and its click sound fired.
- Content loading:
  - A pack chunk that fails to load (offline, or a tab left open across a deploy so its
    hashed chunks are gone) used to stay cached as a rejected promise, so that pack could
    never load again in that tab. Failed loads are now evicted, so they retry.
  - The idle prefetch swallows its errors.
  - If a screen can't be built, the router shows "The path is blocked. The
    tome could not be fetched." with a **Rekindle (reload)** button (EN/PL/UK),
    instead of silently staying on the old screen.

**Verified:**
- Playwright with no service worker and the sports chunk blocked: the error screen
  appears, and after unblocking, Rekindle loads the prompt.
- A sound-on run shows no autoplay warnings.

## 11. New mode: Summon Signs (local pass-and-play party)

**What**
- **Summon Signs** (`#/party`, a home card next to Themes and Archive) is for 2–6 players on
  one device, like Krillion's party mode but with no server.
- **Setup:** name your phantoms, each with a summon-sign rune in a phantom colour (white,
  sunbro gold, blue, red invader, green, violet), and pick a theme or "All themes".
- **Play:**
  - Five prompts, ordered easy → hard.
  - For each prompt, every phantom gets a "the sign glows for you / others look away"
    hand-off screen, then answers privately with the usual 25 s stamina bar.
  - A round reveal shows everyone's answer, tier and points.
- **Echoes:** an answer given by more than one phantom scores half (½), so the rarest
  offering wins, in the spirit of the game.
- The knight sinks to the leader's depth, and rewards and deaths animate per round.
- The final standings rank by depth, with a "‹name› STANDS VICTORIOUS" banner. "Descend
  again" keeps the same names and theme.
- Everything is in EN/PL/UK. `select` elements now use the pixel font.

**Verified:** a full two-player, five-round game in Playwright (setup → hand-offs →
reveal → final). `tsc` and the build are clean.

## 16. Engine test suite

**What**
- `npm test` runs `scripts/engine.test.ts` with Node's built-in test runner, so there is no new
  dependency. There are 12 tests covering:
  - normalization (case, diacritics, punctuation, articles, Roman numerals; unsupported

  - the daily deal is independent of pool order, has 7 unique prompts, is sorted
    easy→hard, and never repeats a prompt on a following day (200 simulated days)
  - `byDifficulty` is stable
  - easter-egg phrase detection (and a plain "t" is *not* the sun) and the zone thresholds

## 19. Accounts and the Covenant subscription (scaffolding, off until keys exist)

**What** (full design and setup: `docs/subscriptions.md`)
- **The rule:**
  - The Daily Descent is always free.
  - Themes, Endless Abyss, Summon Signs and Archive allow **3 free runs per local day**
    (`VITE_FREE_RUNS`).
  - Beyond that, the player needs to sign in (`VITE_GATE=login`) or hold an active paid **Covenant of
    the Abyss** subscription (`VITE_GATE=subscription`, the default).
- **Client:** tiny `fetch` wrappers instead of supabase-js.
  - Magic-link and Google sign-in, with implicit-flow tokens taken from the URL hash before the
    router reads it.
  - Token refresh and a profile read (RLS: own row only).
  - Stripe Checkout and Customer Portal via Edge Functions, plus polling after a successful checkout
    while the webhook lands.
- **UI (EN/PL/UK):**
  - the "The embers are spent" paywall (sign-in first, then Join)
  - an Account section in Settings (email, Covenant status with renewal date, runs
    left, Manage subscription, Sign out)
  - a "2/3 free descents left today" line on the home screen
  - forms in modals now focus their first field
- **Server:**
  - `supabase/migrations/0001_profiles.sql`: `profiles` with RLS and a new-user trigger.
  - Deno Edge Functions: `create-checkout`, `customer-portal` and `stripe-webhook` (verifies the
    signature, mirrors status and period end).
  - Redirect URLs come from the server-side `SITE_URL`, never from the request.
- **Off by default:** with no `VITE_SUPABASE_*` keys, nothing changes for players.
  `VITE_ACCOUNTS_MOCK=1` gives a fake local backend for development.
- `.env.example` documents every variable. `.env` is git-ignored.

**Verified:** with a mock build in Playwright, three free runs → the fourth shows the paywall →
magic-link sign-in → Settings shows the account → Join → the run starts. The production
build (no keys) is unchanged, `tsc` is clean, and 12/12 tests pass. The Edge Functions are
**not** deployed or executed. They need your Supabase and Stripe keys (see the final section).

## 26. Authoritative game server: answers, timer and quota enforced server-side

Your decisions (2026-10-30): paid access only after signing in, and it must persist; Google
sign-in; achievements and a leaderboard for signed-in players; 19,99 PLN or 5 EUR;
enforcement on the server.

**What**
- **`supabase/functions/game`** is the authoritative game server. The answer lists live only
  there. The browser asks for one prompt at a time, sends a guess, and receives the verdict
  plus the reveal lists. Actions: `start`, `prompt`, `answer`, `state`, `stats`,
  `leaderboard`, `profile`.
  - **Quota:** 3 non-daily runs per UTC day without full access (402 `quota`).
  - **Daily:** one run per player per day. It's a unique index in the database, restarting
    resumes the same run, and a finished daily can't be replayed. The day must be within ±1 of the server's.
  - **Timer:** the clock starts when the prompt is served, and re-requesting doesn't reset it.
    Answers after 25 s plus 3 s grace score 0, and double submits are rejected (409).

  - The same engine, packs and dealing logic as the browser: `npm run functions:sync`
    copies them into `supabase/functions/_game` (generated, git-ignored).
- **Anonymous accounts:** every player silently gets an anonymous Supabase account, so
  quotas and the one-daily rule hold before sign-in. Signing in later keeps the same account
  (client work in the next entry).
- **Database (`0002_game.sql`):**
  - `runs`, `turns` and `achievements`, with RLS so players can only read their own rows and
    nobody can write from the browser
  - `profiles` gains `display_name`, `full_access` (a lifetime purchase that persists),
    `purchased_at`, `purchase_currency` and `is_anonymous` (kept in sync by triggers)
- **Achievements** (`src/achievements.ts`, shared): 16 Dark Souls-flavoured achievements (Undead
  Pilgrim, Kindled, No Death, Hollowed, Lord of Cinder, Way of the Pilgrim, Undying,
  Abysswalker, Age of Dark, Estus Hoarder, Lorekeeper, Soul Collector, Soul of Cinder,
  Sunbro, Spectrum of Souls, Centurion of the Pit). The server evaluates them from runs
  it recorded itself, and only for signed-in players.
- **Leaderboards:** daily (by day) and Endless (each player's best), top 50 plus your rank.
  Only signed-in players appear. Names are validated (2–24 letters, digits, `_ . ' -`). Players
  without a name show as "Unkindled #ABCD".
- **Payments:**
  - `create-checkout` refuses anonymous players. It takes `pln` or `eur` from a single Stripe
    Price (PLN base plus an EUR currency option).
  - The Price itself decides the mode: one-time → lifetime "full version" (`full_access`),
    recurring → subscription.
  - The webhook grants access on `checkout.session.completed` and on
    `async_payment_succeeded` (Przelewy24 and other delayed methods). It revokes access on
    `charge.refunded` and mirrors subscription status.
- The helpers are split into `_supabase.ts` and `_stripe.ts`, so the game function never loads Stripe.
- Local stack: `supabase/config.toml` enables anonymous sign-ins and sets the local redirect URLs.
